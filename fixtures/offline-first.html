Designing for the Tunnel

By Priya Raman

Every commuter knows the moment: the train drops underground, the progress bar freezes, and the app you were relying on becomes a blank rectangle. Offline support is often sold as an edge case. For a reading app, it is the main case.

Cache the shell, store the content

An application shell is the part of the interface that never changes: layout, styles, scripts. A service worker can serve it from cache so the app opens instantly with no network. The content itself belongs in a local database, where it can be queried without a round trip.

self.addEventListener('fetch', (event) => {
  event.respondWith(
    caches.match(event.request).then((hit) => hit ?? fetch(event.request)),
  );
});

Be honest about state

Offline-first does not mean pretending the network is always there. Tell people what is saved, what is waiting, and what cannot work yet. A small, calm indicator beats a spinner that never resolves.

The best offline experience is one the reader never has to think about.

Test it the unglamorous way: switch on airplane mode and use the product for a day. The gaps you find will be the ones your users find on the metro.
