Setting Type for Screens

By Jonas Lindqvist

Good typography on a screen starts with a number that nobody writes down: the measure. Somewhere between sixty and seventy-five characters per line, the eye can return to the next line without hunting. Push past ninety and readers lose their place; shrink below forty and the text stutters.

Leading and rhythm

Line height is the second dial. Body text wants roughly one and a half times the font size, a little more for long lines and a little less for short ones. Headings tighten up, because large type needs less air to hold together.

A working stylesheet

The following is enough to make most articles comfortable to read:

article {
  max-width: 68ch;
  font: 1.15rem/1.7 Georgia, serif;
  margin-inline: auto;
}

Notice what is missing: no fixed pixel widths, no tiny gray text. Everything scales with the reader's own settings, which is the point.

Numbers and detail

Tabular figures keep columns of numbers aligned, which matters in dashboards and reading statistics. Proportional figures look better in running prose. Choose each on purpose.
