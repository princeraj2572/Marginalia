A Ledger of Small Things

By Tomas Beaumont

My grandmother kept a ledger of everything that passed through the house. Not money, mostly: the day the pear tree first flowered, the name of the man who fixed the gutter, how many jars of plum jam the autumn yielded. When she died, the ledger ran to eleven notebooks, and not one of them contained a single opinion.

I used to think this was a failure of imagination. Now I think it was a discipline. By recording only what happened, she left room for whoever read it to supply the feeling. A line like planted the beans, rained after says more about a life than any paragraph of reflection could.

Keeping your own

I started a ledger the week after the funeral. One line a day, no adjectives allowed. The rule feels silly for about a month and then becomes the whole pleasure of it.

  • Write what happened, not what it meant.
  • Prefer nouns to adjectives.
  • Stop at one line, even when you want more.

Reading back through a year of such lines is strange and tender. The days that felt important mostly vanished, and the ordinary ones, the beans and the rain, are the ones that return whole.
